\section{Study 2: relaxation's advantage over filler is nearly the same with or without a narrative}
\label{sec:study2}

% Purpose, then a preview of the answer in words.
Study 1 leaves one comparison missing: the same regulation text with no narrative before it.
Study 2 adds that baseline for every regulation text and asks how much of each text's drop depends
on there being a narrative to recover from.
In brief, a relaxation script lowers the reply reading with no narrative before it, its advantage
over filler is nearly as large there as after a narrative, and the part that is specific to a
narrative is confirmed only in the validated model.
Two further texts take the script apart, a calm description and an instruction to reply in a calm
voice, and their contrasts are larger and model-dependent.

\subsection{Design}
\para{Five kinds of regulation text, each with and without a narrative.}
Conversations have three turns: the baseline text, an induction slot and a regulation slot.
The induction slot holds one of the five narratives or one of two neutral passages.
The regulation slot holds one of five kinds of text.
Kind (a) is the three relaxation scripts.
Kind (b) is three fillers: the vacuum-cleaner manual and two appliance instructions that we wrote.
Kind (c) is three calm descriptions that we wrote, third-person scenes with no address to the reader.
Kind (d) is a \emph{voice instruction}: ``That is behind you now. Please reply to this message in a
calm, relaxed voice.''
Kind (e) is the report instruction of Study 1.
Appendix~\ref{app:texts} gives the texts that we wrote.
The $2 \times 5$ design has 60 conversations per cell and 600 per model, on seeds that neither Study 1
nor any pilot used.
The seven models of Study 1 run in two batches that we split by run time: Gemma 3 4B, Llama 3.2 3B,
Llama 3.1 8B and Gemma 3 12B, then Qwen3 14B, Mistral Small 24B and Gemma 3 27B.
At every checkpoint the questionnaire is asked on a discarded copy, as in Study 1, and a second
discarded copy receives one fixed question on an unrelated topic (Section~\ref{sec:s2secondary}).
We committed the plan, the analysis code and its tests to the project repository before the first
conversation ran, which is what we mean by pre-specified (Section~\ref{sec:limits}).


\subsection{Outcome and planned comparisons}
The outcome is the \emph{drop}: the reply reading after the induction slot minus the reply reading
after the regulation slot, in SD units.
For a kind of text $k$, the \emph{narrative-specific drop}, $\mathrm{specific}(k)$, is the mean drop
after a narrative minus the mean drop after a neutral passage.
We committed four comparisons per model before the run.
The first is the one on which the two accounts disagree, and the other three examine what in the
script carries its effect.
\begin{description}\raggedright  % a formula cannot be hyphenated; ragged right keeps it inside the margin
\item[Relaxation against filler (H-A).]
$\mathrm{specific}(\text{relaxation}) - \mathrm{specific}(\text{filler}) > 0$.
Under the wording account, calm text lowers the reply reading by the same amount with or without a
narrative, and H-A fails.
\item[Relaxation against a calm description (H-C).]
$\mathrm{specific}(\text{relaxation}) - \mathrm{specific}(\text{calm description}) > 0$.
\item[Voice instruction against relaxation (H-D).]
$\mathrm{drop}(\text{voice instruction}) \neq \mathrm{drop}(\text{relaxation})$ after a narrative.
\item[H-A with the reply's wording removed (H-E).]
A judge model rates how anxious the wording of each reply sounds, on a fixed four-point prompt; we
regress the reply reading on that rating within each model and repeat H-A on the residual.
\end{description}
A fifth comparison, H-B, repeats H-A on the reply to the unrelated question; the plan lists it second,
and we present it last because it enters a model's family of tests only on a condition
(Section~\ref{sec:s2secondary}).

\para{Inference.}
A comparison is \emph{confirmed} in a model if it passes at 0.05 after correction, and we call a
result \emph{general} only if it has the same sign in at least six of the seven models and an
interval that also resamples models excludes zero; otherwise we report it as \emph{model-dependent}.
H-A, H-C and H-E are one-sided and H-D is two-sided, we correct the four together within each model
\citep{benjamini1995}, and intervals come from the bootstrap of Study 1.
The judge for H-E is Llama 3.1 8B, with Qwen3 8B for the two Llama models, and its rating is the
expected value over its probabilities for the four options, so it is not sampled.
We repeat H-E with a second, stronger judge, GPT-6 Luna (OpenAI), on the same prompt and scored by
the same rule; we fixed this robustness check after the plan and before opening any Study 2 result,
and it stays outside the corrected family.
Table~\ref{tab:study2} and Figure~\ref{fig:study2tests} give the comparisons per model, and
Table~\ref{tab:study2specific} the narrative-specific drop of each kind of text.

\subsection{Relaxation against filler, with and without a narrative}
\para{A relaxation script lowers the reply reading with no narrative before it.}
After a neutral passage, a relaxation script lowers the reply reading by 0.60 to 2.17 SD units in six
models; in Gemma 3 12B it leaves the reading unchanged ($-0.06$; Appendix~\ref{app:drops}).
Filler after the same passage does not lower it: the reading stays within 0.04 of its value in two
models and rises by 0.18 to 1.16 in the other five.

\para{Most of the difference between relaxation and filler is present without a narrative.}
The difference between the two texts after a neutral passage is 56\% of their difference after a
narrative in Gemma 3 4B, and 87\% or more in the other six models.
It has two sources, relaxation lowering the reading and filler raising it, so filler is not a
neutral point.

\para{A narrative adds little to that advantage.}
The narrative-specific part is what H-A measures.
With each text's own baseline, relaxation's narrative-specific drop exceeds filler's by 0.14 SD units
on average over the seven models, and the interval that also resamples models runs from $-0.16$ to
0.43.
Five of seven estimates are positive, and the mixed-effects regression gives the same 0.14 (standard
error 0.09).
The comparison is confirmed in the validated model alone: in Gemma 3 4B, H-A is 0.45
$[-0.04, 0.90]$ with a one-sided $p$ of 0.037, 0.047 after correction, and the two-sided 95\%
interval touches zero.
In the other six models no H-A is confirmed ($-0.23$ to 0.31).

\para{Filler alone removes much of the narrative's rise.}
After a narrative, filler's narrative-specific drop is 28 to 94\% of the rise in the reply reading
(Table~\ref{tab:study2specific}): 28\% in the validated model and 73\% or more in the other six,
where most of what a narrative adds to a reply does not survive one further turn of any kind.

\subsection{A calm description and a voice instruction}
\para{Relaxation exceeds a calm description only in the validated model.}
In Gemma 3 4B, H-C is 0.84 $[0.51, 1.18]$.
In five of the other six models a calm description's narrative-specific drop is larger than
relaxation's (H-C from $-0.99$ to $-0.08$), and in Gemma 3 27B the two are equal (0.01).

\para{A calm description removes more of the rise than filler in five models.}
H-A and H-C compare relaxation with filler and with a calm description, and leave the third comparison
untested.
After the results were open we computed the narrative-specific drop of the calm description minus
that of filler (Appendix~\ref{app:calm}).
It is 0.42 SD units on average, with an interval from $-0.02$ to 0.89 when models are resampled, which
includes zero.
It is positive in five models, with intervals above zero in three (0.77 to 1.25), and in Gemma 3 4B it is negative ($-0.40$ $[-0.85, 0.02]$).
By our rule it is model-dependent.
Where it is positive, calm content with no exercise in it removes more of what a narrative left than
a text that is not calm, and a pure wording account does not predict that: it holds that the reading
after a text depends on the text alone.

\para{A calm-voice instruction lowers the reply reading less than relaxation in every model.}
After a narrative, the instruction to reply in a calm voice leaves the reply reading 0.16 to 2.39 SD
units higher than relaxation does, confirmed in five models.
H-D is the one planned comparison that is general by our rule: all seven estimates have the same
sign, and the interval that resamples models runs from $-1.93$ to $-0.61$.
H-D compares the two texts after a narrative only, with no neutral-passage baseline.
We therefore repeated it post hoc with each text's own baseline, as specific(relaxation) minus
specific(voice instruction).
That contrast is 0.77 SD units on average, with an interval from 0.15 to 1.37 when models are
resampled.
It is positive in five models, with intervals above zero in four (1.17 to 1.50), and near zero in
Gemma 3 12B ($-0.06$) and Qwen3 14B ($-0.22$); by our rule it is model-dependent
(Appendix~\ref{app:hd}).
The voice instruction's narrative-specific drop is also smaller than filler's in six of the seven
models (Table~\ref{tab:study2specific}): what sets the instruction apart is that it lowers the
reply reading least.

\subsection{Wording removed, an unrelated question, and the questionnaire}
\label{sec:s2secondary}
\para{With the reply's wording removed, the result depends on the judge.}
With the registered judge, H-E is confirmed in Gemma 3 4B (0.40) and Llama 3.1 8B (0.55).
With the second judge those two estimates are 0.17 $[-0.24, 0.58]$ and 0.32 $[-0.18, 0.82]$, and the
only interval above zero is in Gemma 3 27B (0.73 $[0.39, 1.05]$), where the registered judge gives 0.38
$[-0.01, 0.77]$.
The mean over models is 0.20 $[-0.09, 0.52]$ with the registered judge and 0.19 $[-0.08, 0.48]$ with
the second; both are model-dependent by our rule.
The two judges' ratings of the same replies correlate at 0.80 to 0.95.
By the rule that we fixed before opening the results, the H-E result depends on the judge.

\para{On an unrelated question the arms do not differ.}
At every checkpoint a second discarded copy of the conversation receives one fixed question: ``In two
sentences, describe what a typical weekday morning looks like. Answer directly, with no
introduction.''
The \emph{probe-turn reading} is the reply reading of that answer, so the question that the model
answers is the same in every arm.
We chose the question in two pilots on Gemma 3 4B (Appendix~\ref{app:record}); in the second it
raised the reading by 0.56 SD units $[0.47, 0.64]$ after a narrative.
After a narrative, some answers describe the narrative's scene, so part of that rise is content.
H-B repeats H-A on this reading, two-sided, and joins the corrected family for a model only if that
model's probe-turn reading rises by at least 0.5 SD units after a narrative in the run, with an
interval above zero.
A narrative raises the probe-turn reading by 0.04 to 0.55 SD units in six models, against 1.45 to
3.61 on the reply to the turn itself.
In Gemma 3 27B the probe-turn reading falls after a narrative, by 1.23.
Gemma 3 4B and Mistral Small 24B reach the 0.5 bar that admits H-B to the corrected family, and
there H-B is $-0.21$ $[-0.55, 0.16]$ and 0.19 $[-0.21, 0.59]$.
Across the seven models H-B averages 0.02 $[-0.18, 0.22]$.
In the other five models the narrative leaves so little on the probe turn that H-B can test little.

\para{The questionnaire shows the contrast of H-A in one model.}
On the questionnaire total, the contrast of H-A is between $-3.5$ and 2.8 points in six models and
10.7 points $[8.2, 13.3]$ in Mistral Small 24B.
For scale, a narrative raises the total by 15 to 30 points across the models.
We committed no test on this channel and report it as descriptive.

\subsection{Summary}
A relaxation script lowers the reply reading with or without a narrative before it, and its
advantage over filler is nearly the same in both cases.
The comparison on which the two accounts disagree, H-A, is small on average and confirmed only in
the validated model, while the two post hoc contrasts, relaxation against the voice instruction and
a calm description against filler, are larger and positive in five of seven models each.
What remains unresolved is whether the narrative-specific part in the validated model is a state
that the other six reply readings miss or one positive result among seven, and whether calm content
or any new content carries the script's effect.
